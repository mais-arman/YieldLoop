## 2. Complete rework accounting without double-counting material

Separate rework recording is currently missing. Add it to the job recording and reconciliation workflow.

Rework must be represented as a traceable material disposition, not merely a note or a percentage.

Implement the following accounting model:

**Issued material = Good output + Scrap + Reusable offcuts + Pending rework + Unexplained variance**

Important rules:
- A quantity pending rework is counted once as pending rework.
- When rework succeeds, move that quantity from pending rework to good output.
- When rework fails, move that quantity from pending rework to scrap and record the relevant reason.
- Support partial rework resolution when the quantity allows it.
- Never count a resolved rework quantity as both pending rework and final output.
- Prevent resolving more than the outstanding pending quantity.
- Preserve the existing decimal precision and units. Do not silently convert material units.
- Record who made each change, when, the quantity, the disposition, and relevant reason/notes in the audit history.
- Keep historical corrections traceable; do not erase history to make totals balance.

Update the job form, job detail, reconciliation summary, relevant reports, and translations so users can see pending and resolved rework clearly.

A job must not be shown as fully accounted for while unresolved rework is omitted from the reconciliation. Keep unexplained variance visible and distinct from scrap. Apply the current closure rules consistently and update them only as necessary for this accounting model.

Create migrations safely and include tests for normal rework, partial recovery, failed rework, invalid quantities, corrections, closure, and reconciliation.

Do not change existing IK-202 or IK-203 results simply to accommodate this new feature. Their supplied data contains no rework.

- Show original quantity, remaining quantity, reserved quantity, and available-to-reserve quantity accurately.
- Keep an immutable movement/audit history showing the action, quantity, user, timestamp, source lot, and destination job when applicable.
- Use database transactions and suitable locking or concurrency controls to prevent simultaneous operations from overspending the same stock.
- Validate quantities, material compatibility, and any relevant material attributes already represented in the application.
- Prevent negative stock, over-reservation, over-consumption, invalid releases, and unauthorized actions.
- Keep voiding/correction rules safe. Never erase an offcut lot or its history if it has already been moved or consumed; use an audited reversal or correction process where appropriate.

“Available” means recorded as available in the system. It does **not** prove that the material is physically present, defect-free, or suitable for a particular future job. Make this distinction clear in concise UI wording.

Use this permission baseline:
- Operators can view permitted inventory information and register offcuts through their authorized production workflow.
- Supervisors and Managers can perform the appropriate stock-management operations.
- Only Managers can perform privileged catalogue/administrative actions or exceptional corrections, unless the existing documented policy requires stricter rules.

Use existing material and job fields; do not invent compatibility guarantees when the data is unavailable.

Add complete tests for partial movements, invalid operations, permissions, concurrent operations where test infrastructure supports them, history, and reconciliation integrity.

## 7. Preserve and verify the production calculations

Keep the core definitions consistent across job cards, dashboards, reports, and comparisons:

- Good yield = good output / issued material.
- Scrap rate = scrap / issued material.
- Offcut rate = reusable offcuts / issued material.
- Accounted quantity = good output + scrap + reusable offcuts + pending rework under the updated reconciliation model.
- Unexplained variance = issued material minus all accounted disposition quantities.

Do not count reusable offcuts as scrap. Do not silently classify unexplained variance as scrap. Do not calculate meaningful rates when issued quantity is zero.

Keep totals based on compatible units and materials. If a report aggregates quantities, prevent invalid aggregation across incompatible materials or clearly require one selected material. Use weighted totals rather than averaging individual job percentages.

Make the report date-filter behavior explicit: if filtering by job creation date and displaying the jobs’ current balances, say so accurately. Do not label current balances as historical “as-of” balances unless historical snapshots actually support that claim.

Verify these supplied development scenarios without creating duplicate jobs:
- **IK-202, CT-2:** 1,200 kg issued; 900 kg good; 180 kg setup/layout scrap; 60 kg damaged scrap; 60 kg reusable offcuts. Total scrap 240 kg, scrap rate 20%, good yield 75%, variance 0 kg.
- **IK-203, CT-2:** 1,000 kg issued; 820 kg good; 100 kg scrap; 80 kg reusable offcuts. Total scrap 100 kg, scrap rate 10%, good yield 82%, variance 0 kg.
- Comparison: IK-202 scrap rate is 10 percentage points higher and yield is 7 percentage points lower than IK-203.

Keep the 100 kg scrap reason for IK-203 unspecified unless its actual source record provides a reason. Do not invent one.

These are development validation cases, not permission to seed production data. Do not overwrite or duplicate existing records. Use isolated test fixtures for automated tests.
