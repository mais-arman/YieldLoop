# YieldLoop — Full Requirements and Functional Audit

I need a comprehensive audit of the existing YieldLoop application against the business requirements below. This is an internship project proposal for Ibn Khaldoun Printing and Publishing: a cutting material yield tool that tracks where issued board goes, separates true scrap from reusable offcuts, validates job weight balance, and compares cutting jobs.

**Important: Audit first. Do not implement changes yet.**

Inspect the actual codebase, database models, API endpoints, frontend routes and components, authentication, role permissions, and existing Preview data. Run safe tests where possible. Do not assume a feature works just because a page or button exists.

Do not reset passwords, create or delete users, modify business records, change database schemas, or expose credentials. Do not switch Preview databases or alter the existing demo data. If a test requires modifying records, use isolated test data and explain what you intend to do first.

## 1. Business requirements and acceptance criteria

### A. Job card and material issue
An operator must be able to:
- Open a job card and identify the job.
- Select the material and cutting machine.
- Record the total material issued in kilograms.
- Access the product specification, sheet size, planned yield, and cutting instructions attached to the job, where these records are available.

### B. Job-close recording
Staff must be able to record the final material quantities separately:
- Good cut product.
- Scrap by reason, including setup/layout scrap and damaged-material scrap.
- Reusable offcuts.
- Rework, recorded separately where supported.

The application must clearly distinguish scrap from reusable offcuts. Reusable offcuts must not be counted as scrap.

Inspect how rework is represented and included in the material balance. Identify any ambiguity in the business rules rather than inventing a calculation.

### C. Weight balance validation
The application must reject an unbalanced job and explain the discrepancy.

The core balance is:

Issued material = Good output + Scrap + Reusable offcuts

For the supplied cases, no separate rework quantity is specified. Check whether the implementation validates the balance correctly, handles missing values, prevents invalid negative quantities, and avoids rounding or unit-conversion errors.

Do not assume the application validates this rule just because it displays a total.

### D. Yield and scrap calculations
Use these explicit definitions:

- Good yield (%) = Good output ÷ Issued material × 100
- Scrap rate (%) = Total scrap ÷ Issued material × 100
- Reusable offcuts (%) = Reusable offcuts ÷ Issued material × 100

All percentages must use issued material as the denominator. Verify the formulas in the backend as well as the frontend, and confirm that reusable offcuts are excluded from scrap.

### E. Job comparison
The manager must be able to compare two jobs on the same machine and inspect the underlying records behind the difference.

The tool should display the relevant quantities, yields, scrap rates, reusable offcuts, and scrap reasons. Confirm whether machine matching is enforced or whether the UI merely allows users to select a machine.

### F. Roles and permissions
The intended users are:
- Cutting operator.
- Production supervisor.
- Manager.

Inspect what each role can actually view and do, including job creation, editing, closing, viewing reports, comparing jobs, and managing users if applicable.

Check both frontend access controls and backend/API authorization. A hidden button alone does not establish secure role-based access.

Do not assume particular permissions without finding evidence in the existing implementation.

### G. Largest scrap category and optional AI summary
For IK-202, the system should identify setup/layout scrap as the largest category at 180 kg.

If AI assistance exists, inspect whether its summary is based on recorded job data and whether it distinguishes an observed loss category from a confirmed root cause. It must not claim that setup or layout caused the loss without supporting operator evidence.

AI is optional, not a mandatory requirement.

## 2. Mandatory test cases

Verify the following reference cases using existing tests, read-only inspection, or isolated test data. Do not alter the existing business records.

**Case 1 — IK-202**

Issued board: 1,200 kg
- Good product: 900 kg

**Case 2 — IK-203**

Issued board: 1,000 kg

- Total scrap: 100 kg
- Reusable offcuts: 80 kg

Expected results:
- Total accounted for = 1,000 kg
- Balance difference = 0 kg
- Good yield = 82%
- Scrap rate = 10%
- Reusable offcuts rate = 8%

The source does not provide a breakdown of IK-203 scrap by reason. Do not invent one.

**Comparison — IK-202 versus IK-203**

Both jobs use cutting machine CT-2.

Expected:
- IK-202 scrap rate = 20%
- IK-203 scrap rate = 10%
- Difference = 10 percentage points, not merely “10% higher”
- IK-202 has 140 kg more total scrap, although its issued material is 200 kg greater.
- The 60 kg of reusable offcuts in IK-202 must not be counted as scrap.
- The manager must be able to inspect the records explaining the comparison.

**Negative and edge cases**

Check safely, using isolated test data where necessary:
- A job whose accounted material is less than the issued material.
- A job whose accounted material exceeds the issued material.
- Zero or missing issued weight.
- Negative quantities.
- Missing scrap reasons or duplicate reason entries.
